# Ohio Childcare: Emergency Preparedness Drills and Documentation (2026)

This post is informational and operational, not legal advice. Confirm exact drill types and frequencies for your program type in the OAC and ODJFS guidance.

## The inspection reality

Inspectors generally want evidence of two things:

- you practiced the drills required for your program type
- you documented them consistently

If your documentation is inconsistent, it looks like drills did not happen.

## The “one page” drill record (what it should contain)

Keep a single drill log format for everything:

- Drill type (fire, tornado, etc.)
- Date + start time + end time
- Rooms/groups included
- Staff leading the drill
- Headcount verification method
- Notes: what went well, what to improve
- Corrective actions and completion date

## Make drills routine instead of a scramble

Operational approach:

1. Put drills on the calendar as recurring events.
2. Assign an owner.
3. Store logs in one place.
4. Review monthly for gaps.

## What to do this week

- Create a “Drills” folder: `Safety/Drills/YYYY/`
- Standardize the template once.
- Decide who signs off and how quickly after the drill.
